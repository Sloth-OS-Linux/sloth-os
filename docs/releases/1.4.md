## Your desktop, your way

Sloth Center → Appearance (and the welcome wizard) now has seven taskbar layouts:

- **Windows 10** and **Windows 11**, as before.
- **Windows 7:** the classic two-column Start menu, task buttons with the windows' names, and "show desktop" in the
  corner.
- **macOS-like:** a top bar and a floating dock. New: the **Launchpad**, every app as a full-screen grid that grows
  out of its dock button, and a small **system menu** under the logo at the top left, as on a Mac (About This
  Computer, System Settings, App Store, Force Quit, Sleep, Restart, Shut Down, Lock Screen, Log Out).
- **Ubuntu-like:** a top bar and a dock on the left. New: **Show Apps**, the desktop shrinks to a small live picture
  at the top with your open windows, and every app sits below it in pages.
- **ChromeOS-like:** a shelf with a round launcher in the corner that opens the apps as a bubble (the search, your
  recent apps, then every app), and **quick settings** in the other corner: a small pill with the network, Bluetooth,
  the sound and the clock, which opens Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, do not disturb, airplane mode, power saver, the volume, the
  brightness, a screenshot, lock and shut down.
- **Floating:** a short bar in the middle that gets out of the way of your windows.

The macOS-like and Ubuntu-like layouts come with four workspaces and a switcher in the top bar. The full-screen app
grid can be turned off in Appearance if you prefer the Start menu. Mac-like and Ubuntu-like desktops set up before get
the app grid by themselves at the next sign-in.

**Six new color schemes with real color:** Ocean Blue, Forest Green, Sunset Orange and Grape Purple (dark), Rose and
Amber (light), next to the seven calm ones. The text stays easy to read on all of them.

## The installer

- **Advanced: choose the partitions yourself.** On the disk page you can open KDE Partition Manager, arrange the disk
  as you like, then pick a system partition and an EFI partition. Everything else on the disk stays as it is.
- **A search field** in the language, keyboard and time zone lists: type "buda" and Europe / Budapest is there.
- **The encrypted disk's password prompt says what it waits for** ("Type your disk password, then press Enter"), in
  the language you chose in the installer.
- **Installing next to Windows a second time** now replaces the earlier Sloth OS instead of saying there is no room.

### Installing next to Windows again: read this first

Sloth OS only makes Windows' partition smaller when Windows is fully shut down, otherwise Windows could lose files
the next time it starts. Windows often isn't: fast startup keeps it half asleep, a restart from Windows skips its
disk check, and the installer then asks you to go back to Windows. To get it right in one go:

1. Unplug the Sloth OS USB stick.
2. Start Windows and wait until you see the desktop (if a disk check is due, it runs now).
3. Hold down **Shift** while you click **Shut down**. This always shuts Windows down completely.
4. Plug the USB stick back in and start the computer from it.

If an earlier Sloth OS is already next to Windows, its place is reused and Windows is not touched at all, so none of
this is needed.

## Smaller changes

- **Num Lock is on from the start**, on the login screen too, so a PIN of digits can be typed on the number pad.
- **The screen locks after 15 minutes** instead of 5, and Sloth Center → Account and security sets Never / 5 / 15 /
  30 minutes and whether to lock after sleep.
- **Old NVIDIA graphics cards** (older than the GeForce RTX 20 series) stay on the standard version with the open
  driver; NVIDIA's current driver does not support them. A computer that was switched by mistake goes back by itself.
- **Going back to an older version sticks:** picking an older version pauses the automatic update until you choose to
  update again.
- **Ralink Wi-Fi cards** (RT28xx/RT30xx) no longer drop off the network after a while.
- **Tab in password fields** goes on to the next field, not to the show-password button.

Every new text is available in all 50 languages.

## Known rough edges

- **Installing next to Windows can keep asking for a disk check on some computers,** even after the steps above. The
  sure way around it: in Windows, open Disk Management (right-click the Start button), right-click drive C: →
  Shrink Volume, and free at least 64 GB. The installer then puts Sloth OS into that free space and does not touch
  the Windows partition at all, so no disk check is needed.
- On some old NVIDIA cards (for example a GeForce GTS 450) the open driver can make the screen flicker. We are on it.

## Requirements

A 64-bit PC that starts in UEFI mode, 4 GB of memory (16 GB for games), 64 GB of disk space, and a USB stick of
8 GB or more. Feedback: Sloth Center → Feedback.
